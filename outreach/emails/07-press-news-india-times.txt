Dear Editor,

Pandit Samir Chatterjee, who has performed at the Nobel Peace Prize ceremony in Oslo and at the United Nations General Assembly, joins internationally acclaimed sitarist Jayanta Banerjee at the Abrons Arts Center on Sunday 30 August for Silsila: Resonance, presented by Ruh Arts Foundation.

The evening also marks the first live performance from Banerjee's new album Echoes to Sky, and features a sitar duet between Banerjee and his disciple Tehreem Khan honouring the guru-shishya tradition that has carried this music across generations. It closes with A Dialogue Between East and West, a finale Banerjee composed for seven musicians.

Would you consider a listing or a short preview? The artists are available for interview, and press images are attached for your use.

ABOUT THE EVENING (100 words, for listings)

Silsila means continuation; resonance is the note that lingers after it is played. Presented by Ruh Arts Foundation, this evening traces the living journey of South Asian classical music. Internationally acclaimed sitarist Jayanta Banerjee and Pandit Samir Chatterjee, a virtuoso of the Farrukhabad tabla gharana, open in conversation. A sitar duet between Banerjee and his disciple Tehreem Khan honours the guru-shishya bond that has carried this music across generations. The night closes with A Dialogue Between East and West, a collaborative finale composed by Banerjee, uniting sitar, tabla, violin, bansuri, keyboard and dholak.
