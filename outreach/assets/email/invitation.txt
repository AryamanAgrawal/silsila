Dear friends,
We're thrilled to invite you to Silsila: Resonance — an evening celebrating the living journey of South Asian classical music.
Silsila means continuation. Resonance is the note that lingers long after it's played. Together, they capture a night where centuries of tradition meet the spontaneity of the present moment.
Presented by Tehreem Khan (Ruh Arts Foundation), the evening brings together masters, a disciple, and a new generation of students in a program of improvisation, rhythmic dialogue, and profound stillness.
The evening features:
Shri Jayanta Banerjee on Sitar & Pandit Samir Chatterjee on Tabla — two internationally acclaimed masters in conversation
A Sitar Duet — Jayanta Banerjee and his disciple Tehreem Khan, honoring the guru-shishya (teacher-student) bond across generations
A Dialogue Between East and West — a collaborative finale composed by Jayanta Banerjee, anchored by Pandit Samir Chatterjee’s tabla, uniting Tehreem Khan (sitar), Sikandar Rahman (violin), Pranav Shikarpur (bansuri), Shiva Kannan (keyboard), and Aditya Pillai (dholak) in a celebration of musical exchange
Shri Jayanta Banerjee is an internationally acclaimed sitarist, composer, and multidisciplinary musician trained in the Rampur and Maihar gharanas under gurus including Pt. Santosh Bandopadhyay and Pt. Robi Chakraborty. He has performed at prestigious venues worldwide, including the Hollywood Bowl, Royal Festival Hall, and the National Centre for the Performing Arts, and collaborated with legends such as Pt. Birju Maharaj, Pt. Chitresh Das, and Vidwan Vikku Vinayakram. A composer, educator, and cultural curator, he is the founder of "Sambandh," a festival reviving the intimate Baithaki tradition through interdisciplinary collaborations across music, dance, and theatre.
Pandit Samir Chatterjee is a virtuoso tabla player of the Farrukhabad Gharana who has performed at the Nobel Peace Prize ceremony in Oslo and the United Nations General Assembly. He has accompanied many of India's greatest musicians, including Pt. Ravi Shankar, Ud. Vilayat Khan, and Pt. Bhimsen Joshi, and collaborated with Western artists ranging from Branford Marsalis to the Boston Philharmonic. Based in the New York area, he is the Founder-Director of Chhandayan, author of the comprehensive A Study of Tabla, and serves on the faculty of the Manhattan School of Music and the University of Pittsburgh.
About Ruh Arts Foundation Ruh Arts Foundation is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to honoring and safeguarding the Subcontinent's classical arts — cultivating artist-led, community-oriented spaces where mastery, ancestral knowledge, and rigorous practice converge to ensure these endangered traditions endure for generations to come.
